# Competition Submission Brief

**Hatch — Targeted competition entries during and after the grant period.**

The ENGenious Sparks Grant explicitly supports student participation in local and international competitions. This document outlines the specific competitions Hatch will submit to, the relevant deadline windows, the angle of each submission, and what supporting materials from this repository will be reused.

The applicant intends to enter at least three of the competitions below over the 12-month grant period.

---

## 1. James Dyson Award

**Tier:** International, with national rounds. The single highest-leverage target.

**What they reward.** Engineering and design projects by current students or recent graduates that solve a real problem. Past winners have included low-cost medical devices, environmental sensors, accessibility tools, and humanitarian technology. Singapore has its own national round.

**Why Hatch fits.** Photogenic hardware + clear problem + measurable impact + sustainability angle (proactive intervention vs. reactive fogging). The James Dyson Award judges respond strongly to projects with a *built and tested* deliverable; Hatch's Phase 3 field deployment is exactly the kind of evidence that scores well.

**Timing.** Annual cycle. Singapore round typically opens around March, closes around July of each year. The applicant targets the cycle that closes after Phase 3 completion (M07–M09 of the grant), allowing real field data to be included in the submission.

**Submission angle.** "A SGD 100 sensor that catches dengue at the source — co-designed with town councils, validated in HDB conditions."

**Re-usable materials from this repo.** Whitepaper, dashboard screenshots, field deployment photographs (to be generated in Phase 3), node-bench prototype photos, the alert pipeline video demo.

---

## 2. IEEE Region 10 Humanitarian Technology Conference (R10-HTC)

**Tier:** Regional academic conference with a student paper track.

**What they reward.** Engineering applied to humanitarian or development challenges, with a clear technical contribution and ideally field validation. Strong tradition of accepting embedded-systems and IoT work targeting public-health problems.

**Why Hatch fits.** Dengue is a regional public-health priority across IEEE Region 10 (Asia-Pacific). The acoustic-sensor + environmental-fusion architecture is a publishable technical contribution. The annotated literature review already covers the related work that a paper introduction would cite.

**Timing.** Annual conference, typically October–December. Paper deadlines six months prior. Targeting the cycle after Phase 3 field data is in hand.

**Submission angle.** Short technical paper (6–8 pages, IEEE format) titled something like *"Multi-modal edge-AI sensing for proactive Aedes source reduction in dense urban environments."*

**Re-usable materials from this repo.** The whitepaper structure maps almost directly to a conference paper draft; the literature review becomes the related-work section.

## 3. Asia Smart App Challenge

**Tier:** Regional smart-city / app innovation competition organised by IDA-equivalent agencies across Asia.

**What they reward.** Practical applications of smart technology to urban living, public services, or community welfare.

**Why Hatch fits.** Smart-city framing — the dashboard layer of Hatch is an operational tool for town councils, which positions the project squarely in the smart-city / govtech space. Singapore's Smart Nation push provides additional thematic alignment.

**Timing.** Varies by host country; Singapore typically runs an event annually. Targeting the cycle after Phase 4 (dissemination phase) of the grant.

**Submission angle.** Emphasis shifts from the hardware to the *operator dashboard* and the *town-council workflow integration*. The deployed sensors are presented as the data source for a smart-city analytics tool.

**Re-usable materials from this repo.** Dashboard prototype, alert log from field deployment, integration diagram, town-council workflow brief.

## 4. NTU College of Engineering / NTUitive innovation challenges

**Tier:** Internal NTU competitions and innovation showcases.

**What they reward.** NTU student-led innovation with potential for impact or commercialisation, ranging from undergraduate research showcase events to NTUitive's startup-incubation tracks.

**Why Hatch fits.** Internal NTU presence is a strategic plus for both the entrepreneurship-major narrative and for ongoing visibility within the engineering college. NTUitive in particular can provide guidance on the commercialisation pathway beyond the grant.

**Timing.** Multiple events per year. The applicant will target at least one NTU showcase per grant period semester.

**Submission angle.** Adapted per event; the entrepreneurship-major narrative and the clear B2G commercialisation path (town councils, NEA) is the unique differentiator.

**Re-usable materials from this repo.** Entire repository.

## 5. NEA innovation challenges (when applicable)

**Tier:** Singapore government agency innovation calls.

**What they reward.** NEA periodically opens innovation calls for technologies that support its mandates (vector control, waste management, environmental monitoring). These are highly aligned with Hatch.

**Why Hatch fits.** Directly aligned to NEA's source-reduction priority. Provides a path to potential operational adoption beyond the grant.

**Timing.** Opportunistic — depends on NEA call cycles, which are not strictly periodic.

**Submission angle.** Hatch is positioned as a complementary technology to the Gravitrap network rather than a replacement, with a clear value proposition for the source-reduction inspection workflow.

**Re-usable materials from this repo.** Whitepaper, field study results, dashboard.

## 7. Cross-cutting submission strategy

Some practical considerations that apply across multiple submissions:

**Visual identity consistency.** All submissions reuse the same visual identity (the landing page's editorial design language). This is a small thing that signals operational maturity to judges who review many submissions.

**Modular materials.** The repository structure means a competition submission can usually be assembled in 2–4 hours by copying the relevant whitepaper sections, generating one or two new figures (e.g., a field-deployment photo, a dashboard screenshot), and adapting the framing. Submissions do not require new technical work.

**Honest framing.** Every submission states that the field deployment uses **funded sensors deployed by a Y1 solo applicant**, not a research team's pre-built infrastructure. This honesty is a strength, not a weakness — judges who have reviewed many overstuffed submissions notice and value it.

**Open-source commitment as a competition asset.** Several competitions (Hackaday Prize especially, but also James Dyson and IEEE in their evaluation rubrics) value open-source release. Hatch's commitment to release firmware, hardware, ML pipeline, and dataset at the end of the grant period is a material differentiator.
