01 / Problem

A surveillance system designed to count adults, not catch breeding early.

Endemic disease
Public health priority
Source reduction gap

Dengue is endemic in Singapore. The National Environment Agency (NEA) operates one of the most sophisticated vector-control programs in the world — over 64,000 Gravitraps deployed at roughly one per twenty households, weekly manual species identification, the Wolbachia release program, GIS-based risk mapping, and inter-agency dengue task forces.

And yet, the country recorded more than 13,000 cases in 2024 with 17 deaths, and saw a peak outbreak above 35,000 cases in 2020. The economic cost of dengue to Singapore from 2010–2020 has been estimated at SGD 148 million. The reason this happens despite the surveillance is structural: existing tools count adult mosquitoes after they have already emerged.

An Aedes aegypti egg can develop into a biting adult in as little as 5–7 days under Singapore's tropical conditions. The Gravitrap network samples weekly. Source-reduction inspections by NEA officers are driven by complaints or by Gravitrap signals that are already 7–14 days behind the breeding event. By the time a hotspot is flagged, the next generation is already biting.

What Singapore's program lacks is not surveillance. It is a continuous, hyper-local, breeding-site-level signal — one that can tell a town council "this drain has been stagnant for four days, conditions are favorable, and we are now hearing Aedes activity around it" before the next generation emerges.

02 / Insight

Stop asking "did I hear a mosquito?" Start asking "is this site producing one?"

Multi-modal fusion
Conditional inference
Source-reduction native

Acoustic mosquito classification is a crowded research area. Multiple groups since 2022 — Altayeb, Zennaro & Rovai; Kimutai & Förster; the MosquIoT team; the HumBug Project at Oxford; the VECTRACK commercial system in Portugal — have demonstrated TinyML or smartphone-based wingbeat classification at 90–94% accuracy. The basic question of "can a microcontroller distinguish Aedes from Culex from sound?" is answered.

But every one of these systems treats acoustic detection as the primary signal, which forces them to fight two losing battles: high false-positive rates in noisy outdoor environments, and disconnection from any actionable intervention — knowing a mosquito flew by tells you nothing about where it came from.

Hatch reframes the problem. We use environmental sensing as the primary signal — water presence, stagnation duration, temperature — and acoustic ML as confirmation. The device only listens when conditions are favorable for breeding. A high-confidence alert requires both modalities to agree: stagnant water present for at least N days at favorable temperature, AND Aedes wingbeats detected nearby.

This shift has three consequences. First, the false-positive rate collapses, because a single noisy chirp does not trigger an alert; the environmental gate must be open. Second, the alert itself is actionable — it identifies a specific breeding site, not just an area where mosquitoes were heard. Third, the system aligns with NEA's stated doctrine that source reduction is the key strategy, rather than competing with the Gravitrap network for the same data NEA already collects.

03 / Approach

Two sensing modes. One high-confidence alert.

Mode 01 — Always-on

Environmental Watch / μW class

The node sleeps for 99% of the time, waking every 5 minutes to sample its environmental state. A capacitive sensor reports water presence; a stagnation timer accumulates dry-flow intervals; a BME280 reports temperature and humidity. Together they compute a breeding-favorability score on-device.

ESP32-S3 in deep-sleep, ~50 μA average Capacitive water-presence + flow timer BME280 (T, RH, P) Breeding score updates every 5 min
Mode 02 — Triggered

Acoustic Confirmation / TinyML

When the favorability score crosses threshold, the node wakes its INMP441 microphone and runs a quantized 1D-CNN trained on the HumBug wingbeat corpus, fine-tuned on Singapore field recordings. Optional hydrophone monitors larval surfacing activity. Detection only counts as an alert when both modes concur.

INMP441 MEMS mic, 16kHz sampling ~40KB INT8 1D-CNN, < 200ms inference Optional piezo hydrophone, drain-mounted Alert = ENV ∧ ACOUSTIC

05 / Roadmap

Twelve months. Three phases. Demonstrable deliverables at each gate.

M01 – M03
Phase 1
Bench prototype

Single-node v0 on the bench

Component selection, firmware skeleton on ESP32-S3, sensor characterization in lab conditions, baseline TinyML model trained on HumBug data via Edge Impulse. Gate: a node powered from solar that wakes on env. trigger and runs acoustic inference on a recorded sample.

M04 – M06
Phase 2
Enclosure + comms

Field-ready hardware

IP66 enclosure, weatherproof connectors for capacitive probe and hydrophone, LoRaWAN integration with a campus gateway, time-series backend, dashboard MVP. Gate: three nodes operating outdoors for two weeks unattended.

M07 – M09
Phase 3
Field study

Pilot deployment

Deployment of 10 nodes across selected NTU and adjacent HDB locations with town-council coordination. Field data collection for SG-specific fine-tuning of acoustic model. Gate: validated alert pipeline with at least one confirmed-and-acted-upon breeding site.

M10 – M12
Phase 4
Dissemination

Paper, competition, open-source release

Technical report + dataset release; submission to James Dyson Award, IEEE R10 Humanitarian Tech, Asia Smart App Challenge; public open-source release of firmware, hardware files, and dashboard. Gate: at least one external competition entry submitted.
