01Problem

The most sophisticated dengue program in the world is still one generation behind.

Endemic disease
Surveillance latency
Source-reduction gap
Sub-block resolution

Dengue is endemic in Singapore. The National Environment Agency operates one of the most sophisticated vector-control programmes on Earth: 64,000+ Gravitraps deployed at roughly one per twenty households, the Wolbachia release program, weekly lab-based species identification, GIS risk modelling, inter-agency task forces, and aggressive source-reduction inspections.

And yet, the country recorded 13,655 cases and 17 deaths in 2024, with a peak outbreak above 35,000 cases in 2020. The dengue economic burden is estimated at SGD 148 million between 2010 and 2020. The 2025 case count dropped sharply, but public-health authorities have publicly warned that a shift in dominant viral serotype could trigger another outbreak within a year.

The reason this happens despite the surveillance is structural: every existing detection modality counts adult mosquitoes after they have emerged. Gravitraps catch gravid adults. Inspections look for current standing water, but only when an inspector physically arrives. The GIS risk model is downstream of these.

An Aedes aegypti egg becomes a biting adult in five to seven days under tropical conditions. The Gravitrap network samples weekly. By the time a hotspot is flagged, the next generation is already biting. What Singapore's program lacks is not surveillance — it's a continuous, hyper-local, breeding-site-level signal.

02Insight

Stop asking "did I hear a mosquito?"
Start asking "is this site producing one?"

Multi-modal fusion
Conditional inference
Source-reduction
native deployment

Acoustic mosquito classification is a crowded research area. Since 2022, multiple groups have demonstrated TinyML wingbeat classification at 90–94% accuracy: Altayeb & Zennaro on Arduino-class hardware, the HumBug Project at Oxford, MosquitoSong+ for noise robustness, the commercial VECTRACK system in Portugal. The basic question of "can a microcontroller distinguish Aedes from Culex from sound?" is answered.

But every one of these systems treats acoustic detection as the primary signal, forcing them to fight two losing battles: high false-positive rates in noisy outdoor environments, and disconnection from any actionable intervention — knowing a mosquito flew past tells you nothing about where it came from.

Exuvia inverts the signal hierarchy. We use environmental sensing as the primary signal — water presence, stagnation duration, temperature, humidity — and treat the acoustic stage as confirmation. The device only listens when conditions favour breeding. An alert requires both modalities to agree: stagnant water present at favourable temperature, and Aedes wingbeats detected nearby.

The architectural shift
Three consequences follow. False positives collapse, because no isolated chirp triggers an alert. Alerts become actionable — each one is tied to a specific physical breeding site, not just "an area where mosquitoes were heard." And power scales, because the expensive ML stage runs only when conditions justify it.
03Approach

Two sensing modes. One high-confidence alert.

Edge node design
Always-on / triggered
Adaptive baseline
Temporal voting

The node spends 99% of its time in deep sleep. Every five minutes it wakes, samples its environment, updates a stagnation timer, and computes a transparent breeding-favourability score. Only when the score crosses threshold does it activate the microphone and the neural network.

A single classifier event is not enough. The node maintains a rolling window of the last five acoustic inferences; an alert fires only when at least three of the five agree on Aedes presence. This temporal voting eliminates the long tail of single-frame false positives that pure-acoustic systems suffer from.

Mode 01 / Always-on
~50 μA avg

Environmental Watch / μW class

An ESP32-S3 in deep sleep wakes every 5 minutes to read a capacitive water probe, a BME280 (temperature, humidity, pressure), and update a continuous stagnation timer. Together these compute the breeding-favourability score on-device — a transparent, hand-tuned weighted function. No ML in this loop. Failure-resilient and operationally legible.

Mode 02 / Triggered
~110 mA · ~200ms

Acoustic Confirmation / TinyML

When the favourability score crosses threshold, the node powers the INMP441 MEMS microphone and runs a quantized 1D-CNN trained on the HumBug wingbeat corpus, fine-tuned on Singapore field recordings. Mel-spectrogram + PCEN normalization for noise robustness. Four-class softmax over Ae. aegypti, Ae. albopictus, other insects, and noise. An optional piezo hydrophone monitors larval surfacing activity in the water itself.

04Architecture

Edge node → gateway → cloud → operator. Every layer earns its complexity.

Three-tier system
LoRaWAN AS923
16-byte alert packets
Time-series backend

The system has four layers and no superfluous infrastructure. Each node communicates with a LoRaWAN gateway that aggregates uplinks from an entire estate. The gateway forwards to a time-series database; an operator dashboard renders the per-block risk heatmap with drilldown to individual nodes. The data path from "Aedes confirmed at drain B2" to "alert visible to a town-council officer" runs in under thirty seconds.

Edge node
Solar-powered, IP66 housed, deep-sleep design. Indefinite outdoor operation.
LoRa gateway
One per estate. Roof-mounted. AS923 frequency plan for Singapore.
Cloud services
Standard IoT plumbing — broker, time-series DB, risk scoring layer.
Dashboard
Designed for the town-council vector-control officer workflow.

Each entry below corresponds to a peer-reviewed source in the project's annotated literature review. None of the prior systems combine in-situ breeding-site environmental sensing with on-device acoustic confirmation, deployed under a source-reduction operational doctrine. That intersection is the design space Exuvia occupies.

Work Year Contribution Gap Exuvia addresses
Altayeb, Zennaro & Rovai — TinyML wingbeat classifier acoustic 2022 94% accuracy distinguishing Aedes from other species on Arduino-class MCUs with LoRaWAN uplinks. Foundational TinyML demonstration. No environmental context; acoustic signal triggers continuously.
HumBug Project — Sinka et al. (Oxford) dataset 2021 Smartphone-based acoustic capture pipeline; 6,900+ wild mosquito recordings spanning six genera. Reused as the primary training corpus; assumes human-in-the-loop.
VECTRACK (IRIDEON) commercial 2024 Bioacoustic mosquito ID integrated with Biogents Sentinel traps; field-deployed across multiple Portuguese regions. Single-modal; trap-coupled; high per-unit cost; not ambient.
MosquIoT — Pruzzo et al. visual 2024 Computer-vision edge ML for Aedes egg counting in ovitraps; field tested in Argentina. Visual-only signal; still requires deploying a managed trap.
MosquitoSong+ — Wang et al. noise-robust 2024 Deep CNN robust to outdoor noise. Joint species & sex inference. >80% accuracy in noisy field audio. Authors note: deployment on microcontroller-class hardware remains future work.
NEA Gravitrap Network operational 2013— 64,000+ sticky traps deployed island-wide. Weekly manual species ID. Gravitrap aegypti Index (GAI) feeds risk models. 7–14 day latency by design; counts adults, not breeding events.
Exuvia novel 2026— Multi-modal fusion at the breeding site itself. Environment as primary signal, acoustic as confirmation. Conditional-inference power gating. Source-reduction native. — this is the gap —

07Roadmap

Twelve months. Four phases. Demonstrable deliverables at every gate.

M01–M03
Phase 1 / Bench prototype

Single-node v0 on the bench

Component characterisation, firmware skeleton compiling on ESP32-S3, capacitive probe calibration, baseline TinyML model trained on HumBug data via Edge Impulse, end-to-end power profile measured against the design budget.

Gate. Solar-powered node wakes on env. trigger, runs acoustic inference on a recorded sample, transmits an alert packet.
M04–M06
Phase 2 / Field-ready hardware

IP66 enclosures, LoRaWAN, dashboard MVP

Production PCB iteration, IP66 enclosure with cable glands sized for probe and antenna leads, LoRaWAN gateway commissioned on campus rooftop, time-series backend and operator dashboard deployed with mocked-to-real data swap.

Gate. Three nodes operating outdoors unattended for two weeks, full data path visible on dashboard.
M07–M09
Phase 3 / Pilot deployment

Ten-node field study

Five nodes on NTU campus, five in an adjacent HDB estate with town-council coordination. Continuous data capture for Singapore-specific acoustic model fine-tuning. Direct comparison of Exuvia alerts against co-located NEA Gravitrap Aedes Index readings.

Gate. Validated alert pipeline with at least one confirmed-and-acted-upon breeding site by a stakeholder.
M10–M12
Phase 4 / Dissemination & release

Paper, competition, open-source release

Technical report and Singapore field dataset prepared for public release under CC-BY 4.0. Submissions to James Dyson Award, IEEE R10 Humanitarian Technology Conference, and Asia Smart App Challenge. Firmware released under MIT, hardware under CERN-OHL-W.

Gate. At least one international competition submission filed; full open-source release tagged.
